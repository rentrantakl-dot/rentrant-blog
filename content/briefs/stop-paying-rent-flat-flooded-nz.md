Yes. In New Zealand the rent **abates** — stops or reduces — once a rental is destroyed or so seriously damaged that it's uninhabitable. You can also end the tenancy on **two days' notice**, fixed term or not.

That comes from [section 59 of the Residential Tenancies Act 1986](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/0120/latest/DLM95528.html), which almost nobody reads until the ceiling comes down.

---

## What the Section Actually Says

Where premises are "destroyed, or are so seriously damaged as to be uninhabitable," the Act is blunt: *"the rent shall abate accordingly."* Not on request. Not as a favour. It's a consequence of the property's condition.

[Tenancy Services](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/maintenance-and-inspections/damage-and-repairs/what-to-do-after-a-natural-disaster/) gives the plain version: tenants required to move out with all their belongings while repairs are done "won't have to pay rent until the property can be lived in again."

If only part of the place is out of action, the rent abates *accordingly* — one unusable bedroom lowers the rent, it doesn't stop it. There's no formula in the Act, so that number is a negotiation, and a Tribunal decision if the negotiation fails.

---

## Two Days to Get Out

| Who's ending it | Minimum notice |
|---|---|
| **Tenant** | **2 days** |
| **Landlord** | **7 days** |

And this applies to **fixed-term tenancies too**. A fixed term doesn't keep you liable for a house you can't live in.

Where the damage is partial, either party can instead apply to the [Tenancy Tribunal](https://www.justice.govt.nz/tribunals/tenancy-tribunal/) to end the tenancy — granted where it'd be unreasonable to require the landlord to reinstate the place, or unreasonable to make you carry on living there even at reduced rent.

> **Key point:** Abatement isn't the same as withholding rent in protest. One follows from the property's condition; the other is a breach that earns you a 14-day notice to remedy. Get the reduction agreed in writing, and if your landlord won't engage, apply to the Tribunal rather than simply stopping payment.

---

## Get the Proof Now

Tenancy Services says proof of uninhabitability "could take the form of a notice from emergency services or council officials," and that photos of the damage may be used by either party. That official notice is usually created for you in the first day — keep it.

Then photograph every room before anything is cleaned, and put your notice to the landlord in writing so there's a timestamp.
