There's no single number in New Zealand law. Rent has to be unpaid for at least **5 working days** before it counts against you at all — and only the [Tenancy Tribunal](https://www.justice.govt.nz/tribunals/tenancy-tribunal/) can actually evict you.

Your landlord can't. Locks changed, belongings on the lawn, "be out by Sunday" — unlawful, no matter what you owe.

---

## The Actual Timeline

Per [Tenancy Services](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/rent/overdue-rent/):

| Stage | Trigger | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Notice of overdue rent** | Rent unpaid **5 working days** | A formal warning. Each occasion counts separately |
| **14-day notice to remedy** | You owe **less than 21 days' rent** | 14 calendar days to clear arrears *and* keep current rent paid |
| **Tribunal application** | **3 occasions** in a **90-day period** | Landlord applies under **s55(1)(aa)** to end the tenancy |
| **Their deadline** | Filed **within 28 days** of the third notice | A hard date on the landlord's side |

Two things there cut your way.

**Five working days, not one.** Working days exclude weekends and public holidays. Rent that lands late but inside that window doesn't feed the three-notice count.

**The three occasions must sit inside 90 days.** They don't accumulate forever. A notice from five months ago isn't part of a current sequence.

---

## What Makes a Notice Challengeable

Tenancy Services is explicit that you can apply to the Tribunal to challenge a notice, and that you'd need to show either the rent wasn't 5 working days overdue, or the notice didn't meet the requirements.

Each notice has to state the amount overdue, the dates, **your right to apply to the Tribunal**, and **how many qualifying notices you've already been given** in the 90-day period. Three notices where one is defective isn't a sequence of three valid notices.

> **Key point:** Write down the date on every notice the moment it arrives, and photograph it. The whole process runs on dates, and they're the first thing that goes fuzzy under stress.

## Do This Week

Talk to them before they talk to the Tribunal — a repayment arrangement agreed in writing changes the picture entirely, and leaves no public record the way a Tribunal order does. Check whether you qualify for the [Accommodation Supplement](https://www.workandincome.govt.nz/products/a-z-benefits/accommodation-supplement.html) or the [Accommodation Costs Arrears Grant](https://www.workandincome.govt.nz/housing/live-in-home/housing-costs/accommodation-costs-arrears.html); you don't have to be on a benefit for either. And get free advice early from [Community Law](https://communitylaw.org.nz/) — the options narrow once an application is filed.

Being behind on rent isn't the end of a tenancy. It's the start of a process with dates that mostly give you room.
