Rent's due on Wednesday. One of you hasn't put their share in, again, and it's now the third month. You've covered it because the alternative was everyone getting a notice. You're several thousand dollars down and running out of patience.

The frustrating thing when you go looking for help: tenancy.govt.nz doesn't have your answer. Not because the site is incomplete, but because **your situation isn't tenancy law at all** — and once you understand why, the path forward gets a lot clearer.

---

## Why Tenancy Law Can't Help You

The [Residential Tenancies Act 1986 covers tenants and landlords](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/who-is-protected/who-tenancy-law-does-not-protect/) — not flatmates.

Tenancy Services is explicit about it. If a group rents a house together and some of them don't sign the tenancy agreement, the ones who didn't sign **aren't tenants**. They're flatmates, and their living arrangement sits outside the Act entirely. Same if a homeowner rents a room to someone in their own home.

So the Tenancy Tribunal — the body you'd naturally reach for — has no jurisdiction over the arrangement between you and your flatmate. There's no bond process to draw on, no 14-day notice to remedy, none of the machinery that exists between tenants and landlords.

That's the gap, and it's why this problem feels so unusually unsupported when you hit it. The machinery exists for tenancies; your arrangement with a flatmate is a private agreement between two people, and it gets treated like one.

---

## The Two Obligations That Don't Cancel Out

This is the part that catches people, and there's no gentle version of it.

If you signed the tenancy agreement, **you are jointly liable for all of the rent** — not your share of it. [Tenancy Services](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/flatting/) puts it plainly: *"Tenants are jointly responsible for all the rent and any damage, not just their own share."*

And more bluntly still: *"If 1 tenant causes damage to the property or gets behind in paying rent, all other tenants can be held responsible. They may have to pay the debt if the offending tenant does not."*

Which produces this:

| Relationship | Governed by | Who's liable |
|---|---|---|
| You → Landlord | Residential Tenancies Act 1986 | You, for the **full** rent |
| Flatmate → You | Your private agreement | Them, for their share |

Two separate obligations, two separate forums. **Your flatmate not paying you does not reduce what you owe the landlord.**

> **Warning:** Don't withhold rent from the landlord to force the issue. You're the one on the agreement, so the notices and any Tribunal application land on you. If it's already reached that point, our guide to [being behind on rent](/posts/behind-on-rent-nz/) covers the timeline and what to do.

Keep the landlord paid in full. Pursue the flatmate separately. It's galling, and it's the only approach that doesn't make your position worse.

## The Right Venue: Disputes Tribunal

For the flatmate side of it, the forum is the [Disputes Tribunal](https://www.disputestribunal.govt.nz/). [Tenancy Services](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/flatting/) draws the line explicitly: *"Only landlord-tenant disputes are heard by the Tenancy Tribunal. If there are any issues between tenants or flatmates, you can go to the Disputes Tribunal."*

Not the Tenancy Tribunal. This distinction is the single most useful thing in this guide, because filing in the wrong place costs you the fee and the weeks it takes to find out.

The [Ministry of Justice confirmed](https://www.justice.govt.nz/about/news-and-media/news/disputes-tribunal-financial-jurisdiction-set-to-increase/) that on **24 January 2026 the Tribunal's financial jurisdiction increased from $30,000 to $60,000**, so a flatmate rent debt sits comfortably inside it. [Filing fees](https://www.disputestribunal.govt.nz/forms-and-fees/) scale with the claim:

| Claim amount | Fee |
|---|---|
| Less than $2,000 | $62 |
| $2,000 to under $5,000 | $124 |
| $5,000 to $30,000 | $248 |

Most flatmate rent debts land in the first two rows. Fees are non-refundable, though the Referee **can order the other party to pay your filing fee back** if your claim succeeds in whole or in part. Applying online is the quickest route.

What helps you there:

- [ ] **A written flat sharing agreement**, if you have one — this is the difference between a clear claim and an argument about what everyone remembers
- [ ] **Bank records** showing what you paid and what they didn't
- [ ] **The rent ledger** or landlord confirmation of what was owed and when
- [ ] **Messages** — texts, group chats, emails where the arrangement or the promise to pay is discussed
- [ ] **A written demand** to your flatmate for the amount owed, sent before you file

That last one is worth doing properly. A calm, specific, dated message asking for a specific sum by a specific date does two jobs: sometimes it works, and when it doesn't, it's evidence that you tried.

> **Template:**
>
> > Hi [Name],
> >
> > As of [date] you owe $[amount] in rent for [period] — I've covered this to the landlord so the tenancy isn't put at risk.
> >
> > Can you please pay $[amount] to [account] by [date]? If that's not manageable, let me know what you can do and by when, and I'm happy to work something out in writing.
> >
> > If I don't hear back by [date] I'll be applying to the Disputes Tribunal to recover it.
> >
> > [Your name]

## If You Want Them Out

Recovering the money and ending the living arrangement are different problems, and the second one is genuinely murky.

If you have a **written flat sharing agreement**, it should set out how much notice is needed to end the arrangement. Follow it.

If you don't, you're in unclear territory, and this is the point to get free advice rather than act on instinct. Changing the locks, removing someone's belongings, or simply telling them to be gone by Friday can create a much bigger legal problem than the debt you started with. [Community Law](https://communitylaw.org.nz/) and [Citizens Advice Bureau](https://www.cab.org.nz/) both deal with flatting disputes regularly and neither charges.

One thing to be careful about: if the person is actually named on the tenancy agreement, they're a **tenant**, not a flatmate — and removing a tenant is not something you can do at all. That's between the landlord and the Tenancy Tribunal. Check the agreement before you assume which situation you're in.

## What Happens to the Bond

If the leaving flatmate contributed to the bond, that's another private matter — the bond stays lodged with Tenancy Services until the tenancy ends, and it doesn't get partially refunded because one person moved out.

Our brief on [what happens to your bond when a flatmate moves out](/briefs/flatmate-moves-out-bond-nz/) covers the change-of-tenant process, and [your bond rights](/posts/your-bond-rights-nz/) covers the end-of-tenancy side.

The practical point: don't let a departing flatmate's bond contribution become the informal settlement of their rent debt without writing down what was agreed. That's how the argument restarts a year later.

## The Decision That Caused This: Who's on the Agreement

Every flat makes this choice at the start, usually without noticing, and it determines everything above.

**Everyone signs the tenancy agreement.** All of you are tenants. All of you are jointly liable to the landlord for the full rent — so a non-payer still creates a shortfall the rest of you must cover. But you're all inside the Residential Tenancies Act, all named on the bond, and nobody is dependent on one person's goodwill for their housing.

**One person signs, the rest are flatmates.** The signer carries the entire legal relationship with the landlord. The others have no tenancy rights at all — no bond protection, no notice periods, no Tenancy Tribunal. And the head tenant carries 100% of the risk while the flatmates carry none of the legal standing.

| | Everyone signs | One head tenant |
|---|---|---|
| Liability for full rent | Shared among tenants | **Head tenant alone** |
| Flatmates' tenancy rights | Everyone has them | **None** |
| Venue for disputes between you | Disputes Tribunal | Disputes Tribunal |
| Bond protection | All named | Head tenant only |

Neither is risk-free, and the head-tenant arrangement is often not chosen so much as inherited — someone signed a lease three years ago and people have rotated through since.

If you're the head tenant, understand what you're actually holding: full liability, with recovery from your flatmates depending on a private agreement and your willingness to enforce it. If you're a flatmate, understand that your housing security rests on someone else's tenancy, which can end without you having any say in it.

> **Key point:** If you're currently a flatmate and the arrangement is stable, ask about being added to the tenancy agreement. It requires the landlord's agreement and it isn't automatic — but it converts you from someone with no rights into a tenant. Our [change of tenant](/briefs/flatmate-moves-out-bond-nz/) brief covers the process.

## The Fix That Only Works Beforehand

Nearly every flatting money dispute traces back to the same root: nothing was written down.

Tenancy Services publishes a free [flat/house sharing agreement template](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/assets/forms-templates/Flat-house-sharing-agreement.pdf). It covers rent shares, bills, bond contributions, notice periods, and what happens when someone leaves.

It takes twenty minutes to fill in with a new flatmate. It is also, at the Disputes Tribunal, the difference between "here is what we agreed" and "here is what I say we agreed."

If you're setting up a flat now, our guides on [renting with flatmates](/posts/renting-with-flatmates-nz/) and [your rights as a flatmate in Auckland](/posts/flatting-in-auckland-your-rights-as-a-flatmate/) cover the wider picture — including who should be on the tenancy agreement, which is the decision that determines everything above.

## What This Comes Down To

- Flatmates aren't covered by the **Residential Tenancies Act** — that's why tenancy.govt.nz has no answer for you
- Signed tenants are **jointly liable for all the rent**, not their share
- **Keep paying the landlord in full** — the two obligations don't offset
- The venue is the **Disputes Tribunal**, not the Tenancy Tribunal
- **Written demand first**, then file
- Check whether they're a **flatmate or a tenant** before trying to move anyone out
- Next flat: **fill in the sharing agreement** on day one

Sorting this out is slow and mildly humiliating and you shouldn't have to. But the money is recoverable, and the people who get it back are the ones who kept the landlord paid, kept the receipts, and went to the right tribunal.
